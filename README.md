# Direct Action

A triage tool for the El Niño summer of 2026–27, between Brunswick and the Melbourne CBD. It shows which lives are in danger over the coming months, matches each one to a project brief that has worked somewhere else, and turns the brief into a W.I.S.H. poster with the places nearby that can host it or pay for it.

By [novel.global](https://novel.global). Open source under the MIT licence. Made on Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Country.

## Use it

- **On the web:** publish this folder with GitHub Pages (see below), then open the address.
- **On a computer:** download this folder and open `index.html` in a browser.
- **Offline:** once the page has loaded from the web, the app, the last sightings and the map tiles you have viewed stay available without a connection. On a phone, add it to the home screen.

Drag to move the map, pinch or scroll to zoom, and tilt to raise the land. Tap an icon to open its card. Tap empty ground, long-press it, or right-click it to add a record there.

The map carries no words. Each kind of animal has its own colour: birds blue, mammals purple, insects pink, spiders and snails brown, reptiles green, frogs and fish teal. Plants are small and appear only close up. An orange ring marks danger in the months ahead: thin for severe, thick for extreme.

## NOW

- **The warning.** The El Niño outlook for twelve months, as a strip of months shaded in orange by degree of danger. The first three months are the Bureau of Meteorology's forecast. The next three are modelled, because El Niño is expected to persist through summer into autumn. The rest are marked as having no outlook yet. Under the strip are the unseasonable windows: early heat while flying-fox pups are born, dry flowering, hot nights and drought. Tap a month to triage that stretch of three months; the map and the lists follow.
- **Five in greatest need.** The Grey-headed Flying-fox, the Common Ringtail Possum, the Bogong Moth, the Eastern Long-necked Turtle and the Common Eastern Froglet. Each moves its own way, on the page and on the map, where its past iNaturalist sightings gather; a soft patch shows the ground it is known from. Each row gives its degree of danger, what it is short of where it lives, and the brief that answers it.
- **Local emergencies right now.** Animals hurt in the last twelve hours, found dead in the last two days, or lost in the last three, with the call or the search area one touch away.
- **Noticing.** The other lives most in danger over the chosen months, with their degree and when it peaks.
- **Briefs.** The three briefs that best fit the lives in danger now, each shown with the life it is for and the businesses that can carry it.
- **Gigs and gatherings.** Gigs carry a hug icon and come first. Links to the Triple R gig guide and Resident Advisor's Melbourne listings sit underneath.

## STORIES

- **The tools first.** The field list and the guide, then six downloads: the posters, businesses, records, briefs and field list as CSV, and a blank poster to fill in by hand.
- **The posters, top twenty.** Each shown as the poster itself, ranked by votes. One vote per device; a second touch takes it back. An unfunded poster can be hosted or given to from here.
- **Groups already caring.** Friends of Royal Park, Friends of Moonee Ponds Creek, Friends of Merri Creek, CERES, the West Brunswick Community Garden and Food Forest, and Gardens for Wildlife Merri-bek. On the map each shows as the patch of ground it cares for. Touch a patch for what they do, how to join, the lives in their care and the briefs that would bring them.
- **Briefs.** Fifty projects that have worked elsewhere, each turned into a brief for here, with a link to its source, the kinds of life it serves and the businesses that can carry it.
- **Businesses.** Every business and brand in the area has a role. The demo shows 200 invented ones, and none of them has signed up.
- **Archive.** Search, sound and motion settings, your signature, the data as CSV, and the sources.

## A card

- **The danger:** the outlook for the chosen months, how hard heat (counted twice) and drought are on this kind of life according to the field list, whether the heat lands while it breeds or in one of the windows, and the canopy where it lives.
- **Three needs, chosen for its kind.** A flying-fox is read for flowers, fruit and canopy; a possum for canopy, old gums and cats; a bee for flowers, water and baits; an orb-weaver for insects, canopy and baits; a frog for water, insects and runoff; people for canopy, cool rooms and check-ins. Each reading says OK, LOW, MISSING or NEARBY:
  - counts are read against what a patch of the same size usually holds on this map, with a floor;
  - the canopy is read against 40%, the cover at which streets start to cool;
  - water is the distance to the nearest creek, river or wetland;
  - threats are the businesses in the radius whose role harms this kind (baits and sprays, night light, single-use, runoff, microfibres);
  - for a fox or a cat the reading turns round, and shows the native animals AT RISK within its reach.

  Drag the square on the boundary to change the radius, and the readings follow it.
- **The danger** in one sentence, under the name of its window.
- **Anything urgent:** a call, a report, a search, a time and a place.
- **Briefs:** up to three, matched to the kind of life, the months and the businesses in the radius. Each says why it is there: the life it serves, the months it starts in, and the roles nearby that can carry it.
- **Posters:** every poster made for it, shown as the poster itself.
- **Where it goes:** the places in the radius, by role.

## A brief as a poster

IMAGINE THE POSTER turns the card over. The W line ("what we know") is filled from any surprising and urgent truths. The I, S and H lines ("it would be great", "so let's create" and "here is how it works") join-up this noticing towards an action, linked to the suburb and the nearest business that fits. Each line stops where two lines end on the printed poster. Arrows step through the other briefs that fit. Mark places to HOST or GIVE, sign it, and the card turns into the poster.

The poster is A4 and uses as little ink as it can. It opens with CLIMATE EMERGENCY RESPONSE and a warning sign drawn in line, with no solid band, headed by the unseasonable stretch the life faces. Then come the life as photographed (or its kind of life, drawn large in line) and its whole name. Three readings follow, without headings: its worst months and their degree; the danger in one bold sentence, said once; and the canopy where it lives against the 40% it should be, with what it is short of there. Then come the four lines, each under its name in full, as a third of the sheet. The foot holds a QR code back to the record, the project the brief follows, the places it is on show, a phone or email if one was left, and who to call.

## Placing a record

Touch the ground twice, or hold, or right-click. A new record starts as any animal, shown with a paw. Say what you saw in words ("two kittens under a car") and the card names it, counts it and finds its kind. Two rows of choices sit under the words. WHAT HAPPENED offers seen, plant, hurt, dead, lost, need, offer and gather. WHAT IT IS offers thirteen icons: bird, possum, bat, bee, butterfly, beetle, spider, lizard, snake, frog, turtle, plant and other. The words choose first and a touch overrides them. A photo is kept small on the device. A phone number or an email can be left, and is optional; social media handles are refused.

## Businesses

Each business has one role. Roles marked ON NOTICE are highlighted; the others are worth backing.

- **On notice:**
  - single-use waste: responsible for the litter in its radius
  - new clothing: overconsumption, emissions and landfill
  - microfibres
  - rat poison
  - night light
  - runoff
  - roaming cats
- **Worth backing:**
  - repair: clothes, bikes and fans kept in use
  - reuse
  - local labels: small runs, known makers, traceable cloth
  - studios: designers and makers who can take a brief
  - artist spaces
  - co-ops
  - owner-run places
  - markets
  - pop-ups
  - growers
  - vets

Each role links to the kind of life it touches most, with one sourced fact. A business card shows the radius it answers for (400 m), the life it touches most, the briefs it can carry, and a sign-up sheet to print. Nothing is owed. Forty-three of the fifty briefs can be carried by a studio, a label, a repairer, a reuse shop, a clothing shop or an artist space.

| File | What it holds |

| `marks.js` | Every icon and its word, shared by the app and the guide |
| `app.js` | Behaviour |
| `style.css` | Look, on screen and on paper |
| `index.html`, `field.html`, `guide.html` | The app, the field list and the guide |
| `sw.js` | The offline cache |
| `vendor/` | MapLibre GL JS, qrcode-generator, Poppins and IBM Plex Mono, kept here so nothing loads from a code CDN |

Update `DA_OUTLOOK` in `config.js` when the Bureau issues a new long-range forecast. There is no build step for anyone who edits these files, and there are no accounts and no trackers. What people add stays on their own device, and everything can be downloaded as CSV from the archive.

## Events from a sheet

Keep a Google Sheet with the columns `title, start, venue, lat, lng, tags, link`.

- `start` is a date and time, for example `2026-10-09T19:30:00+11:00`.
- `tags` are words separated by spaces: `nature`, `free`, `first-nations`, `sound`, `walk`, `kids`. Add `gig` to mark a gig with the hug icon, and `rrr` or `ra` when the gig is listed by Triple R or Resident Advisor.

Publish the sheet to the web as CSV, and put the link in `EVENTS_URL` in `config.js`.

## Publish on GitHub Pages

1. Create a repository and upload every file in this folder.
2. Go to Settings → Pages → Deploy from a branch → `main`, `/ (root)` → Save.
3. Put the site's address in `PORTAL_URL` in `config.js`, so that printed QR codes lead back to it.
